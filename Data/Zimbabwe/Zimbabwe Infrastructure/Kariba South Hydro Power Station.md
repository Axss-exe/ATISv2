# Kariba South Hydro Power Station

**Infrastructure Type:**  Hydroelectric Power Station
**Operational Status:** `partially_operational`
**Operated By**: [[Zimbabwe Power Company (ZPC)]]
**Value Is**: The largest renewable energy source in Zimbabwe and one of the biggest hydro installations in Southern Africa. Its existence provides lower-cost, lower-carbon baseload power that underwrites the viability of energy-intensive smelting and refining operations. Without Kariba, Zimbabwe would be entirely dependent on expensive diesel and thermal backup, rendering most PGM and ferrochrome operations uneconomic. Lake levels are a systemic climate risk — the 2015–2016 drought cut output by 60%, forcing mine closures. The 2018 expansion (two additional 150 MW units) was financed by China Exim Bank and increased installed capacity but did not resolve water availability constraints.


## Summary

The largest renewable energy source in Zimbabwe and one of the biggest hydro installations in Southern Africa. Its existence provides lower-cost, lower-carbon baseload power that underwrites the viability of energy-intensive smelting and refining operations. Without Kariba, Zimbabwe would be entirely dependent on expensive diesel and thermal backup, rendering most PGM and ferrochrome operations uneconomic. Lake levels are a systemic climate risk — the 2015–2016 drought cut output by 60%, forcing mine closures. The 2018 expansion (two additional 150 MW units) was financed by China Exim Bank and increased installed capacity but did not resolve water availability constraints.

#### Technical Notes: 
1,050 MW installed; ~400–750 MW variable (drought-dependent)

## Construction History

#### Built By

- Original dam and power station built 1955–1962 by Italian consortium [[Impresit]]
- Kariba South Extension (Units 5–6, 300 MW) built 2014–2018 by [[Sinohydro Corporation]]


## Primary Users / Dependents

- ferrochrome producers
- ZETDC (grid frequency regulation)
- rural mining towns (Karoi, Binga)
- SAPP regional pool participants
