# Hwange Thermal Power Station

**Infrastructure Type:**  Power Station
**Operational Status:** `partially_operational`
**Operated By**: [[Zimbabwe Power Company (ZPC)]]
**Value Is**: Zimbabwe's largest coal-fired power station and the backbone of national baseload generation. Without Hwange, the national grid collapses — it supplies roughly 40–50% of dependable grid power. Its existence enables large-scale mining operations (platinum, chrome, gold) that cannot run on intermittent supply. Downtime at Hwange triggers nationwide load shedding, directly halting mine processing plants, smelters, and concentrators. The station's rehabilitation (Units 7–8, 600 MW Chinese-funded expansion) is the single most critical energy infrastructure decision for Zimbabwe's mining sector through 2030.


## Summary

Zimbabwe's largest coal-fired power station and the backbone of national baseload generation. Without Hwange, the national grid collapses — it supplies roughly 40–50% of dependable grid power. Its existence enables large-scale mining operations (platinum, chrome, gold) that cannot run on intermittent supply. Downtime at Hwange triggers nationwide load shedding, directly halting mine processing plants, smelters, and concentrators. The station's rehabilitation (Units 7–8, 600 MW Chinese-funded expansion) is the single most critical energy infrastructure decision for Zimbabwe's mining sector through 2030.

#### Technical Notes: 
1,520 MW installed; ~350–400 MW dependable



## Regulation

#### Regulated By

## Construction History

#### Built By

- Originally built 1983–1987 with Chinese technical assistance (Units 1–6)
- Units 7–8 (600 MW) built 2018–2023 by [[Sinohydro Corporation]] under EPC contract


## Primary Users / Dependents

- Mining companies (Unki, Zimplats, Mimosa, chrome smelters)
- ZETDC (grid stability)
- urban industrial users
- ZINWA (pumping stations)
- NRZ (electrified rail sections)
