## Saltpeter

**Category:** 
**Chemical Formula:** KNO₃
**Global Royalty Range:** 2–5%

## Summary

Potassium nitrate, a naturally occurring nitrate salt. Colorless to white crystalline solid. Highly soluble in water. Strong oxidizer.


## Geological Occurrence

- 1. Evaporite deposits — arid basin accumulations (Chilean nitrate fields, Atacama)
2. Caliche deposits — cemented nitrate-rich surface crusts (Chile, Peru, Bolivia)
3. Cave guano accumulations — bat dung decomposition (historical: Mammoth Cave, Kentucky)
4. Soil efflorescence — arid zone surface crusts (India, Egypt)
5. Synthetic production — reaction of sodium nitrate + potassium chloride (dominates global supply)


## Major End Products

- 1. N-P-K fertilizers (nitrogen & potassium source)
2. Black powder / gunpowder & industrial explosives
3. Food preservative (E252) & meat curing salt
4. Glass manufacturing flux & enamel frits
5. Pharmaceutical diuretics, toothpaste desensitizers & asthma inhalers


## Standard Processing Workflow

- 1. Extraction (mining or synthetic reaction of NaNO₃ + KCl)
2. Dissolution in hot water (80–100°C)
3. Filtration to remove insoluble matter (clay, sand)
4. Fractional crystallization (KNO₃ crystallizes on cooling; NaCl stays in solution)
5. Centrifugation or vacuum filtration
6. Drying in rotary dryers (120–150°C)
7. Prilling or granulation for fertilizer grade
8. Coating with anti-caking agents (clay, talc)


## Common Mining Methods

- • Solution mining (for soluble evaporite beds)
• Open-pit quarrying (caliche/evaporite deposits)
• Cave deposit harvesting (historical, rare; bat guano)
• Surface scraping (soil efflorescence, artisanal)
• Synthetic production dominates (not mining)
