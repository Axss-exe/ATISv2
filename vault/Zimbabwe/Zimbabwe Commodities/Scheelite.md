## Scheelite

**Category:** 
**Chemical Formula:** CaWO₄
**Global Royalty Range:** 2–5%

## Summary

Calcium tungstate mineral, the primary ore of tungsten. Hard, heavy (SG ~6.1), with high melting point. Fluoresces bright blue-white under short-wave UV light.


## Geological Occurrence

- 1. High-temperature skarns — contact zones between granite and limestone (China, Russia, Korea)
2. Greisen deposits — altered granite with quartz-muscovite-topaz (Southwest England, Portugal)
3. Hydrothermal quartz veins — associated with granitic intrusions (Austria, Korea, Zimbabwe)
4. Porphyry copper-molybdenum-tungsten — Chile, Arizona
5. Stratabound replacements — Nevada, California


## Major End Products

- 1. Tungsten carbide cutting, drilling & mining tools
2. Incandescent lamp & vacuum tube filaments
3. Aerospace nickel-based superalloys (turbine blades)
4. Armor-piercing kinetic energy penetrators
5. High-temperature furnace elements, electrodes & nozzles


## Standard Processing Workflow

- 1. Underground or open-pit mining
2. Crushing & grinding
3. Gravity separation (shaking tables, spirals, jigs) — scheelite is dense
4. Froth flotation (fatty acid collectors at pH 9–10)
5. Acid leaching (HCl) to remove calcite & apatite impurities
6. Solvent extraction or ion exchange purification
7. Crystallization to ammonium paratungstate (APT)
8. Hydrogen reduction to tungsten powder
9. Carburization to tungsten carbide (WC)


## Common Mining Methods

- • Underground room and pillar
• Cut and fill stoping (for narrow, steep veins)
• Open-pit (shallow skarns)
• Selective mining due to irregular vein geometry
